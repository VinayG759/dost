The problem: Older people in India are told important things about their own health, then left alone to remember them. The doctor explains the medicines in five minutes, and by evening half of it is gone. Lab reports arrive every few months, and nobody tells them whether the numbers are better or worse than last time.

Who has it, where and how often: My grandmother, Gangamma, is 70 and lives in Bengaluru. She has diabetes and goes for a check-up every three months, alone. She speaks only Kannada and can't read the English on her reports or medicine strips. Her son lives with her, but he works long shifts as a security guard, so she manages her doctor visits and daily medicines by herself, with nobody to ask "what did the doctor say?" Her memory is hazy: she often forgets which tablet is for what, and when she's supposed to take it. Millions of older people across India live like this, and blind and low-vision people face the same wall: phones and paperwork expect you to read.

The voice-first product: Dost is a friend on her phone that she talks to in Kannada, mixed with English the way she really speaks.

Step 1: At the doctor's, the doctor talks to Dost directly: "Metformin 500, raatri oota aada mele, mooru tingalu." Dost repeats it back so the doctor can confirm it understood correctly, and sends her son a photo of the prescription to check. From then on, Dost manages it: it reminds her, answers "Doctor enu helidru?", and tells her when the next check-up is due.

Step 2: She holds up a report. Dost guides her hand ("swalpa edakke… haan, saaku"), reads it and compares: "Sugar 180 ide, kaleda sala 150 ittu. Sihi kammi maadi."

Step 3: She doesn't know her medicines by name; she knows them by how they look. So Dost talks about them the same way. At medicine time it speaks first: "Oota aytha? Eega aa chikka bili gundu maatre tagolli" (the small white round one). Before swallowing it, she shows it to Dost: "Idu sarina?" Dost checks: "Haudu, adhe" or "Illa, adu BP maatre."

Step 4: She says, "Maganige phone maadu," and Dost calls her son.

Step 5: If she hasn't spoken to Dost all day, it asks, "Ivattu maatte aadilla, ella sari ideya?" If she still doesn't answer, it tells her son.

Why voice comes first: Take the voice away and nothing is left. Dost has no menus, no forms and no screen she has to read, so she never has to learn an app. She just talks, the way she'd talk to a friend sitting next to her. That is why it works for someone who can't read English, can't see small text, or can't see at all. Families pay for it, because they can't be with her all day, and Dost can.
